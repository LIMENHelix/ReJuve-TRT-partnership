Structure E · Retainer + tiers — the full growth-operator package

Chris owns the entire non-clinical engine: SEO, website, social media, sales funnels, CRM, coordinators, events, and partnerships — everything except clinical, which stays with the providers (protocols, formulary, patient eligibility, and veto over all marketing claims). A monthly retainer covers the always-on operational scope; the Structure D tier pay rides on top as pure performance.

  • In scope: website + local SEO, Google Business Profile, social content engine, ad campaigns, funnels + CRM (HIPAA-BAA configured), intake automation, coordinators, events calendar, partner program, dashboard reporting.
  • Out of scope (providers): all clinical decisions, claims/formulary, and marketing-claim approval. Ad spend is a pass-through budget Chris manages but doesn't profit from.
The "if it were my business" answer: no agency retainers — the agency stack for this scope runs $5–12k/mo ($60–145k/yr) with zero performance alignment. What an owner still funds: ad spend (pass-through), tools (~$300–600/mo), and task-rate freelance production (editing, design) so Chris's hours go to revenue work, not clip trimming. Structure E costs less than the agency alone — and half of it only exists on results.

6The 645 Club — events, education, and buzz

Gameday in Liberty sells a clinical visit with man-cave decor. The moat here is a real club — and it's bigger than it first looked: our own sports complex (3–4 full basketball courts, hitting cages, gym floor) plus Club 7 at our door. Leagues, camps, and watch parties turn 645 into the default third place for men in the northland. You can't out-franchise a 400-unit chain on clinical sameness — you out-local it on belonging. Events convert guests into free screenings; the app keeps them between events.

House rules — keeps the fun defensible

  • Social lives off the clinical brand: "The 645 Club" is its own name and DBA. Event rosters stay separate from patient records — no health talk, no patient info at events (HIPAA hygiene).
  • Alcohol and cigars happen offsite or venue-permitted only — never served at the clinic. Check lease and liability coverage before anything on-site.
  • Guests are the funnel: every guest gets a free screening voucher and an app download. RSVPs are opt-in marketing contacts.
  • Segment the lists: cigar/whiskey programming and weight-loss programming never cross-promote to the same members.
  • No health claims or testimonials at events without counsel-approved language (Missouri advertising rules still unverified).

Buzz mechanics

  • Every event produces content: 5–10 short clips, member spotlights (signed releases), leaderboard moments.
  • Local sponsors cover costs once the calendar has traction — steakhouse, barber, supplement shop, truck club, gym. Exposure for them, near-zero cost for us, and still no per-referral payments.
  • App challenges feed the events: quiz points and streaks qualify members for VIP access and guest passes.
  • 15 minutes from Gameday cuts both ways — the club has to be worth the drive. Identity and exclusivity ("the northland men's club") are what pull people past a closer option.
Events budget is an input in Section 1 (default $800/mo — roughly one club night plus content). Sponsor offsets can take it near zero once the calendar is established.
